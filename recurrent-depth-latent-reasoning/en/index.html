Looped Transformers and Latent Reasoning: An Architecture Mythos and Astra May Both Use

Following the release of Claude Mythos, OpenMythos speculated that it might employ a Looped Transformer; months later, credible media reported that Astra uses limited Recurrent Depth. Both signals point to the same heating-up direction: models may gain more compute not only by generating longer Chain-of-Thought, but also by repeatedly refining representations within continuous Hidden states. This article starts with these two cases to clarify the relationship between Looped Transformer, Latent Reasoning, and Test-Time Reasoning.

Reading thread: First, see why both Mythos and Astra are associated with Recurrent Depth, then understand the three paths of Test-Time Reasoning. Looped / Recurrent-depth Transformer is not equivalent to all Latent Reasoning; it specifically belongs to Vertical latent recurrence within Latent Reasoning, which involves repeating computation along the network depth.
01

From Mythos Architecture Speculation to Astra Architecture Reporting

Following the release of Claude Mythos in April 2026, Anthropic did not disclose specific architectural details. The community project OpenMythos subsequently proposed a theoretical reconstruction: Mythos may employ Recurrent-depth / Looped Transformer, performing multiple rounds of hidden computation on the same set of Transformer layers. OpenMythos explicitly states that this is an independent hypothesis constructed based on public research and is unrelated to Anthropic; Anthropic's Mythos System Card also does not confirm this architecture.[2][3]

In September 2026, TechCrunch reported on The Information's coverage: OpenAI's Astra model uses limited recurrent depth, also reported as “opaque recurrence.” OpenAI Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki's public response did not explicitly deny recurrence but emphasized that the depth of the computation graph remains constrained and expressed a desire to preserve Chain-of-Thought monitorability. OpenAI has also not yet released complete architecture documentation.[1]

2026 · 04Mythos / OpenMythosThe community translated the Recurrent-depth speculation into code; it is an architectural hypothesis, not evidence of Anthropic's implementation.
2026 · 09Astra ReportingCredible media explicitly reported limited Recurrent Depth; specific layers, loop counts, and stopping mechanisms remain undisclosed.
Introducing two conceptsLooped Transformer + Latent ReasoningThe former describes the architecture, while the latter describes the computational paradigm in hidden space.

How do the two concepts differ?

02

Three Main Computational Paths of Test-Time Reasoning

The common goal of Test-Time Reasoning is to allocate more computation to the current problem while the model weights remain fixed. The primary differences among the three routes lie in where the additional computation occurs, whether new Tokens are generated, and whether the intermediate process is human-readable.

Where does the Looped Transformer fit?

It belongs to Vertical latent recurrence under the second path, Latent Reasoning: it reuses a Shared Core repeatedly along the network depth, increasing sequential computation in the hidden space.

Below, we start with the most familiar Explicit CoT, then move into the two types of Latent recurrence, and finally examine the non-semantic Token route that lies between them.

03

Explicit Reasoning: Expanding Computation with Visible Tokens

Explicit Reasoning writes intermediate steps in language. For every new thought Token generated, the model must pass that Token through the entire network, then add it to the context to generate the next step.

PromptQuestion and context
Thought Token 1"First calculate A"
Thought Token 2"Then check B"
AnswerFinal output

Chain-of-Thought

Generates a step-by-step reasoning trajectory, suitable for math, code, and decomposable tasks.

Long CoT

Allocates more Test-time compute through longer explicit reasoning, but incurs higher latency and Token costs.

Search / Self-consistency

Generates multiple candidate reasoning paths, then uses voting, scoring, or a Verifier to select the result.

Why does it work?

For every additional Token generated by an autoregressive model, it executes one more full network Forward and externalizes the intermediate result as subsequent input. Therefore, CoT is not just "explaining the answer"; it is also a way to add sequential computation steps for the model.

AdvantagesCosts and Limitations
Easy to prompt, train, and deploy; intermediate text can be read by humans or a Monitor; naturally integrates with tool calls and Verifiers.Increases generation time, output Tokens, Context usage, and KV Cache; language expression may force the model to commit to a single path too early.
Suitable for tasks requiring communication, auditing, and reproducible steps.Visible CoT does not guarantee faithful reflection of the true internal computation; the model may also write post-hoc rationalizations.
The Long-Term Position of the Explicit RouteEven as Latent Reasoning becomes stronger, explicit reasoning will likely remain in stages requiring communication, auditing, tool execution, and result verification.
04

Latent Reasoning: Continuing Computation in Continuous Hidden Representations

Latent Reasoning does not require each step to first be converted into natural language Tokens. Continuous vectors can retain richer states than single discrete words and may simultaneously carry multiple candidate directions. Current approaches are mainly divided into Vertical and Horizontal branches.

Vertical latent recurrence: Looping along network depth

Standard Transformers typically stack many Blocks that are structurally similar but have independent parameters. Each Block contains Attention, MLP, Normalization, and a Residual path, but Layer 1 uses parameters θ₁, Layer 2 uses θ₂, up to Layer N using θₙ. The hidden representation passes through in a fixed order once before entering the output layer.

Standard Transformer: Fixed Independent Depth

Token EmbeddingConverts Tokens into vectors
Transformer Block 1 · θ₁Attention + MLP
Transformer Block 2 · θ₂Different parameters
Transformer Block 3 · θ₃Different parameters
… Block N · θₙEach layer typically learns its own function
LM HeadMaps to Next-token logits

Parameter depth and computational depth are roughly bound: N sets of independent Blocks execute N Block computations.

Recurrent Depth: Dedicated Ends + Shared Core

Token EmbeddingConverts Tokens into vectors
Prelude · θₚDedicated input processing layer
Shared Core · θ꜀ · Pass 1Updates Hidden state
Shared Core · Same θ꜀ · Pass 2Refines again
Shared Core · Same θ꜀ · Pass RContinues looping based on budget
↻ SAME WEIGHTS
Coda · θₒDedicated output processing layer
LM HeadMaps to Next-token logits

Unique parameters are primarily determined by the Prelude, one Core, and the Coda; actual computational depth also depends on the number of loops R.

Prelude, Shared Core, and Coda

Prelude

Non-shared input layers before the loop, organizing Embeddings and context into an initial Hidden state suitable for iterative reasoning.

Shared Recurrent Core

Transformer Block or Block group called repeatedly. Each round uses the same set of weights to continue updating the representation; some designs re-inject the original input.

Coda

Non-shared output layers after the loop, converting the Hidden state refined over multiple rounds into a representation suitable for the LM Head.

Why keep the Prelude and Coda?Input understanding, iterative reasoning, and answer formation may require different functions. The ends retain specialization, while the middle Core handles repeatable computation. The specific number of layers, Input injection, Gating, and stopping mechanisms vary across papers.

The Position of Universal TransformerIt is a key source for shared depth and Adaptive Computation, but its original focus is not entirely equivalent to today's Latent Reasoning. Works like Huginn more explicitly interpret Recurrent Depth as Test-time latent compute.

Horizontal latent recurrence: Advancing along latent thought positions

The Horizontal route does not necessarily repeat the same Transformer Core. It is more like replacing the "thought sequence" from natural language Tokens with a series of continuous Hidden states: the current latent state, after model processing, becomes the input for the next latent position, until the model switches back to the language space and generates an answer.

Prompt→h₁→h₂→h₃→Answer

What are Continuous Thoughts?

In standard CoT, each step must first select a discrete Token; Continuous Thought directly uses high-dimensional vectors as intermediate states. A vector does not have to correspond to a translatable sentence; theoretically, it can simultaneously retain multiple candidate directions, confidence levels, and pending information, evolving further in the next step.

Coconut

Training Large Language Models to Reason in a Continuous Latent Space directly feeds the final Hidden state of a step back as the next Continuous Thought. Training uses a staged Curriculum: first using explicit CoT, then gradually replacing intermediate language steps with continuous states. The paper observed behavior similar to retaining multiple candidate paths in parallel on planning tasks. [8]

CODI

Compressing Chain-of-Thought into Continuous Space via Self-Distillation uses the model's own explicit CoT as a teacher, allowing latent thinking to mimic explicit reasoning in both representation and final answer. Deployment can reduce visible reasoning Tokens, but it still relies on language reasoning supervision during the training phase. [9]

Continuous Thoughts

This is a more generalized concept rather than a single model name: intermediate computation is carried by continuous representations, which can be implemented via Hidden-state feedback, self-distillation, compression, or other mechanisms.

ComparisonVertical recurrenceHorizontal recurrence
Where does repetition occur?The same Hidden sequence repeatedly passes through the shared Core along network depth.One latent thought state follows another, advancing along "thought positions".
Typical examplesLooped Transformer, Huginn, Fixed-point ReasonerCoconut, CODI, Continuous Thoughts
Main advantagesDecouples parameter depth from computational depth, making it easy to treat loop count as a compute budget.May retain richer candidate information, reducing the bottleneck of translating every step into language.
Main challengesSerial latency, stability, when to stop, and Weight sharing limiting layer specialization.How to train, how to supervise, when to exit the latent space, and whether latent trajectories are truly causally effective.
Do not treat Continuous Thought as "sentences in the head"Latent states may be partially decodable, but they do not necessarily correspond to complete, step-by-step, faithful natural language reasoning. Performance improvements, decodable patterns, and true causal reasoning are three different issues.
05

Extra Non-semantic Token Compute: Adding Positions Without Natural Language

The third path still adds extra Tokens to the sequence, so the model performs more Forwards; however, these Tokens are not required to represent readable sentences like "calculate A first."

Pause Tokens

Inserts trained Pause embeddings before the answer to provide the model with additional computation positions.

Filler Tokens

Extends the computation sequence with placeholder Tokens that have no task semantics, testing whether "pure extra computation" is helpful.

Learned Planning Tokens

Learns Tokens specifically for planning or compressing intermediate states, without requiring them to correspond to human language.

Difference from Explicit CoTDifference from Latent recurrence
Still adds Token positions, but the Tokens do not need to carry readable reasoning semantics.Does not necessarily feed the Hidden state back as the next Continuous Thought, nor does it necessarily share layers for Depth loops.
Implementation is closer to existing autoregressive infrastructure.KV Cache and sequence length still increase with extra positions.
Why is it listed as a separate third branch?It neither communicates through language like explicit CoT nor is it entirely equivalent to Hidden-state recurrence. Listing it separately avoids conflating "unreadable Tokens" with "latent computation without Tokens."

Interactive | Multiple Loops Do Not Guarantee Continuous Improvement

Select different dynamics to observe how the benefits of additional Loops saturate; in unstable settings, performance drops after looping too deep.

Why is Monitorability the Center of Controversy?

Explicit CoT at least provides a readable language channel; Latent computation is harder to cover with traditional text-based Monitors. However, explicit CoT has never been a complete transcription of internal mechanisms, and readability does not equal faithfulness.

Capability Risk

If Latent compute becomes an easily scalable Runtime dial, labs may sacrifice Monitorability for capability.

Evidence Limitations

Hidden states can be decoded by Probes, or PCA may show beautiful trajectories, but this does not prove that the representation causally dominates the answer.

Reasonable Governance

Measure Monitorability while combining behavioral evaluation, Interpretability, Verifiers, tool permissions, and audits.

Latent Chain-of-Thought? Analysis of Huginn found no stable, clear, step-by-step latent CoT; Chain-of-Thought Monitorability emphasizes that CoT monitoring is imperfect but still valuable, and may be eroded by architectural and training choices. [12][13]

08

Future Route Assessment: Competition, Complementarity, and Convergence

Explicit Reasoning

Assessment: Long-term retention. It is best suited for communication, auditing, tool calling, program execution, and Verifiers; however, it will reduce meaningless verbosity and be triggered more by the model on demand.

Vertical Latent Recurrence

Assessment: Most likely to become a core architectural component. It provides a compute depth Scaling axis beyond parameter count, but must address stability, Adaptive halting, and Serving latency.

Horizontal Latent Recurrence

Assessment: High potential for planning and search tasks. Continuous states may retain multiple candidate directions, but training supervision, interpretability, and Frontier-scale evidence remain insufficient.

Non-semantic Tokens

Assessment: A practical transitional and supplementary route. It is easily compatible with existing Decoders, but may be limited by KV Cache and sequence costs.

Final InsightThe true new trend is not a single architecture replacing the Transformer, but Test-time computation expanding from "generating more text Tokens" into a heterogeneous system: visible reasoning, Hidden state loops, non-semantic computation slots, Search, Verifiers, and Tools dynamically combined according to the task.
